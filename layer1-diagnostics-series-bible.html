Series Bible

Working title

Layer 1 Diagnostics  (alt: Cable Doctor, The Wire Whisperer, Signal Lost)

Logline

A working network engineer intentionally breaks Ethernet cabling in every way real techs encounter in the field, shows exactly what each fault looks like on real diagnostic tools, then repairs and documents it — teaching viewers to diagnose hardware issues instead of guessing.

Audience
  • IT/helpdesk techs who've never had to chase a physical-layer fault
  • Homelab and prosumer network people
  • Trade-adjacent viewers (electricians, low-voltage installers, Network+/A+ students)
  • Secondary: senior engineers watching for war stories (and nitpicking terminations — good for engagement)
Core promise (every episode)

"Here's a real fault, here's what it looks like on the tool, here's the fix, here's how to document it so the next person doesn't repeat your afternoon."

Standard episode shape
  • Symptom — what the end user / helpdesk ticket says
  • Wrong assumption — what gets blamed first and why that wastes time
  • Diagnosis — tester in hand, narrated in real time
  • Reveal — show the actual induced fault
  • Fix — full repair
  • Verify — retest, confirm clean
  • Field Notes — 30–60 sec war story that mirrors the fault
Tone

Confident, unhurried, dry humor. Talk to the viewer like a competent junior tech shadowing you, not a classroom. No sound-effect gimmicks — let tester beeps and crimp-tool clacks be the sound design.

Visual signature
  • Macro/clamp shot on every tester screen — if the viewer can't read the display, the episode fails
  • Consistent lower-third graphic for Symptom / Diagnosis / Fix section markers
  • End card with a one-line "what you learned" recap
Recurring segments
  • Guess the Fault — pause after the diagnostic reveal, invite viewers to guess before the answer
  • Tool Tier Callout — what a cheap tester shows vs. what only a certifier catches
  • Field Notes — closing war story
Equipment list
  • Cheap tone/probe kit
  • Mid-range wiremap/continuity tester
  • Certifier-class tester (Fluke or similar) for split-pair / spec-compliance episodes
  • Punch-down tool, crimper, strippers, keystone jacks, wall plates, patch panel
  • Fiber scope (bonus-tier episodes)
  • Camera macro rig or phone clamp for screen shots
Publishing cadence

Weekly, 8–15 min run times. Grouped as "Season 1: Copper Fundamentals" so the channel reads as a series, not scattered videos.

Episode-by-Episode Outline

EP 01

The Toolkit: What's Actually In My Bag

8–10 min
Fault
None — intro / setup episode
Shots
Overhead flat-lay of tools, close-ups of each tool on a known-good cable, side-by-side cheap tester vs. certifier

Introduce the channel premise, show each tool, run a known-good cable through each one so viewers have a baseline "this is what correct looks like" before faults start. End with the season roadmap.

EP 02

568A vs 568B: Does It Even Matter?

10–12 min
Fault
One end terminated 568A, other 568B
Shots
Split screen of both termination standards on a jack, macro of tester wiremap display, cable pull test

Diagnose via wiremap, explain when this is genuinely a problem (straight-through expected) vs. a non-issue on modern auto-MDIX NICs, re-terminate one end to match, verify.

EP 03

The Wall Plate That Lied

10–12 min
Fault
Improperly terminated wall plate (wrong pin order, untwisted pairs, poor seating)
Shots
Macro on punch-down block, tester wiremap before/after

Symptom = intermittent link. Diagnose with wiremap, open the plate on camera, show the mess, re-terminate correctly, verify, document with a labeled photo for the ticket.

EP 04

Cut Wire, No Warning

10–14 min
Fault
2–3 conductors cut a few inches behind the wall plate
Shots
Tester distance-to-fault reading (macro, hero shot), then cutting open the jacket to reveal the damage

This is the episode to teach TDR distance-to-fault if your tester supports it. Show how the tool tells you where before you ever open the wall. Splice or pull-and-replace, verify, document.

EP 05

The Split Pair That Passes Every Cheap Test

12–15 min
Fault
Correct pins used, but conductors from the wrong twisted pair (e.g. pins 3/6 not from the same pair)
Shots
Cheap tester showing "PASS," then certifier showing NEXT/crosstalk failure — this contrast is the whole episode

The flagship "tool tier" episode. Explains why a link can come up, pass a basic continuity test, and still tank throughput under load. Re-terminate with correct pair mapping, verify on certifier.

EP 06

Five Cables, Zero Labels

10–12 min
Fault
4–5 runs mislabeled at one end
Shots
Wide shot of unlabeled patch panel, tone probe in hand tracing each run, label maker close-ups

Trace-and-tag from scratch using a tone generator/probe. End with a mini-tutorial on a real labeling convention (room-panel-port numbering scheme) so viewers leave with a system, not just "label your stuff."

EP 07

Bend Radius: The Fault You Don't See Until Later

8–10 min
Fault
Cable kinked / stapled with bend radius violated during install; works now, degrades over time
Shots
Before/after certifier readings (install day vs. "6 months later"), macro on the kink itself

Good "why did this randomly start failing" narrative — ties physical damage to gradual signal degradation rather than instant failure.

EP 08

Nail Through the Jacket

10–12 min
Fault
Fastener partially through the cable, causing an intermittent short/ground fault
Shots
Tester showing an intermittent/flaky fault, reveal of the fastener

Teaches diagnosing intermittent faults specifically, which behave differently from clean breaks. Repair via cable replacement (patching over damage isn't code-legal for permanent runs — say so on camera).

EP 09

Patch Panel vs. Wall Plate: Which End Lied to You?

10–12 min
Fault
Same wiremap fault, but on the patch panel side instead of the wall plate side
Shots
Tester at wall plate reads fine, tester at panel reveals the fault

Reinforces a habit: always isolate which end of the run is actually broken before you start opening things up.

EP 10

The Closet With 40 Unlabeled Cables

14–18 min
Fault
Documentation-from-scratch special
Shots
Wide establishing shot of the chaos, sped-up tracing montage, final labeled/organized reveal

Full trace-and-document exercise from zero. Satisfying before/after. Great thumbnail material.

EP 11

Dirty Glass: The Fiber Episode

10–12 min
Fault
Dirty/contaminated fiber connector end-face causing high error rates (bonus tier)
Shots
Fiber scope view of a dirty vs. clean end-face, cleaning tool in use

Introduces fiber troubleshooting as a contrast to copper — same "invisible fault, visible tool" theme.

EP 12

PoE Down, Data Up

10–12 min
Fault
Damaged pair takes down PoE delivery while data link still negotiates (bonus tier)
Shots
Tester/PoE checker readings, camera powering an IP camera or phone to demonstrate the symptom

Useful for anyone doing camera/AP installs — a fault category most general IT content ignores.

EP 13 · FINALE

Field Day: Diagnose 5 Faults, No Prep

15–20 min
Format
Unscripted-feeling capstone — 5 cables rigged by a second person (or randomized beforehand) with faults from the whole season, diagnosed cold on camera

Ties the season together, reinforces the diagnostic process over memorized fixes. Strong finale / rewatch value.
